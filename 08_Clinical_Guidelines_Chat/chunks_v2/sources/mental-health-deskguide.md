## **DEFINITION OF TERMS**

| **Affect** - observable emotional response seen in the person’s non-verbal behavior. |
| --- |
| **Delusions** - Irrational, strong beliefs which have no basis in reality but which cannot be changed by logic or argument. |
| **Disability** – refers to any impairment which may be physical, mental, and developmental or a combination of these. |
| **Fear** - The body’s physiologic and emotional response to a known or recognized danger. |
| **Flight of ideas** – is a symptom that is common in BAD mania patients manifested by expressing many ideas at the same time (jumping from topic to topic). |
| **Grandiose delusions** – abnormal fixed beliefs of expansive ideas such as inflated worth, power, knowledge, identity or special relationship to a famous person. |
| **Hallucinations** - perceptions that occur in the absence of any appropriate external stimuli and could be of any of the five senses. |
| **Illusion** - a false interpretation or perception of a real environmental stimulus that may involve any of the five senses. |
| **Insomnia** – inability to sleep well either difficulty in initiating and/or maintaining sleep or interrupted sleeping pattern. |
| **Impulsivity** - behavior characterized by little or no forethought, reflection, or consideration of the consequences. |
| **Mental Status Examination (MSE)** - a structured way of observing and describing a patient’s current state of mind under the domains of appearance, attitude, behavior, mood, speech, thought process, thought content, perception, cognitions, judgment and insight |
| **Mood** - a normal internal felt subjective emotion that a person experiences most of the time. |
| **Psychotherapy** - use of psychological methods based on regular personal interaction to help a person change and overcome problems in desired ways. It aims at increasing each individual’s wellbeing and mental health. |
| **Psychosis** - A term that refers to a patient presenting with hallucinations, and/or delusions and/or illusions. |
| **Tolerance**: is a state where by the individual who uses alcohol and/or drugs feels the need for markedly increased amounts of the substance to achieve intoxication. |
| **Withdrawal**: are symptoms that occur upon abrupt cessation of excessive alcohol/ substance use. |
| **Dependence**: is an adaptive state that develops when an individual persist in use of alcohol/ substance despite problems related to the use of the substance. |

# FOREWORD

The COVID-19 pandemic has brought great disruptions and immense challenges all across the globe. Uncertainty and major changes to the way we live our lives are having a considerable impact on our mental health. Mental health is an important, integral part of health and well-being of an individual. It is increasingly acknowledged as an important public health concern in the Kingdom of Eswatini. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) 2020, it is estimated that at least 10 percent of the world's population is affected, and that 20 percent of children and adolescents suffer from some type of mental health conditions. Mental illnesses are common in all regions of the world, affecting every community and age groups across all income levels and Eswatini is no exception. Neuropsychiatric conditions rank high, and contribute to the burden of disease in the country. Like other aspects of health, mental illnesses can be impacted by a range of socio-economic factors that need to be addressed through comprehensive strategies of promotion, prevention, treatment and recovery at grass root levels.

Despite growing knowledge on mental illnesses, myths and misperceptions about these disorders persist, leading to stigma and discrimination towards those who suffer from them thus affecting their care and treatment. The Ministry of Health, therefore aims at increasing access to care for people with mental health conditions. The country has only one hospital that provides specialized psychiatric care located in the Manzini region. There are also regional hospitals, health centres and few clinics that provide nurse-led mental health services around the country.

However, there is shortage of specialized mental health personnel, infrastructure and equipment. This presents a challenge for comprehensive mental health service delivery. This document will thus guide health care professionals to provide standardized interventions for common mental health conditions in the country. This will assist all health facilities to address and manage common mental health conditions at their level and refer complicated cases to the National Psychiatric Referral Hospital.

I believe that the use of this desk guideline will result in accessibility, affordability and acceptability of mental health services in our communities and the country at large. This will significantly improve quality of life to people living in the Kingdom of Eswatini and reduce disease burden in the country.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Mental health conditions are a potentially avertable contributor to high morbidity, disability and mortality which requires the attention of policy makers, with suicide as a second leading cause of death among 15-29 year olds (WHO 2020). Approximately one in five people in post-conflict settings have a mental health condition worldwide (WHO 2020). The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), Goal 3 emphasizes on achieving universal health coverage for all by 2030. The Kingdom of Eswatini's constitution (2005) also provides for every citizen's right to access healthcare. Additionally, the Eswatini Mental Health Act of 1978 acknowledges the importance of upholding the rights of individuals living with mental health conditions and these rights include access to mental health care.

The Kingdom of Eswatini like many other countries faces the burden of health challenges. There are insufficient qualified mental health professionals in the country, the majority of who are based at the National Psychiatric Referral Hospital (NPRH) in Manzini. Mental health services are not fully decentralized hence reduce access to care and increase burden to people who need mental healthcare services. There is a dire need to improve access to care, thus necessitating the development of the Mental Health Desk Guide. The development of the mental health case management desk guidelines will help health care professionals to manage and provide standardized interventions for common psychiatric conditions at all levels of health care. This will help in the reduction of burden of disease as mental illness is a risk factor for other health complications. Therefore, dissemination and application of these desk guidelines to all healthcare professionals in different healthcare settings will significantly improve patient access to standardized mental healthcare in the country.

The Kingdom of Eswatini is home to many major diseases; however, it has also experienced a significant increase in mental health related problems particularly with suicide, substance and alcohol use. It is therefore the objective of this Mental Health Desk Guide to bridge the above highlighted gaps in the quality of mental health service delivery in the country.

# 3. **OVERVIEW OF DIFFERENT PSYCHIATRIC CONDITIONS**

## **MAJOR DEPRESSIVE DISORDER**

- It is a common mood disorder characterized by negative changes in feelings, thinking, and behaviour resulting in loss of normal functioning.

## **DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA OF MAJOR DEPRESIVE DISORDER AS PER DSM V;**

- Duration of symptoms is two weeks.
- The patient should present with one or both of the two symptoms (1) and/or (2)
  1. Depressed mood most of the day, nearly every day;
  2. Marked diminished interest or pleasure in all, or almost all, activities most of the day.

## **Plus other four/five symptoms;**

- Disturbed sleeping pattern (insomnia or hypersomnia) nearly every day.
- Disturbed appetite (increase or decrease)
- Weight gain or loss. In children consider failure to make expected weight gain.
- Diminished ability to think or concentrate, or indecisiveness, nearly everyday
- Psychomotor retardation
- Psychomotor agitation (irritability or being angry easily)
- Guilt feeling (self-blame)
- Generalized body pains including headache
- Feeling worthless and hopelessness,
- Fatigue or loss of energy nearly everyday
- Recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal attempt

**Note:**
- children and adolescent may present with irritable mood instead of sad mood
- Few patients may present with psychotic symptoms (hallucinations, delusions or illusions)

- Psychological and social stressors
- Alcohol and substance use issues
- Suicidal thoughts/attempt
- Number of episode? First episode vs relapse
- Medication adherence if it is not a first episode
- Psychological and social support

# SUICIDE AND SELF-HARM

**Suicide** - is the act of intentionally causing one's own death. It usually occurs along with progressive stages from suicidal thoughts, to planning, to attempting suicide, and finally dying by suicide.

- It is a psychiatric emergency preferably all high risk suicidal patients should be attended by a Psychiatrist and the Clinical Psychologist.

**Para-suicide** - an apparent attempted suicide without the intention of killing oneself.

**Most common methods of suicide are;**
- Ingestion of toxic substances
- Shooting
- Hanging
- Jumping from height
- Stabbing
- Overdose of prescribed medication

**Suicide prevention methods:**
- Create awareness about suicide
- Seeking help early if one feels depressed, stressed or feeling emotionally unwell
- Strengthen religious and social connectedness
- Strengthen cultural believes that discourages suicide behavior and support self-preservation
- Practicing a healthy stress free lifestyle
  - Maintenance of good relationships with friends, colleagues and neighbours, parents & children
  - Abstinence from alcohol and illicit drugs use
  - Encourage problem solving, conflict resolution and non-violent handling of disputes
- Restricted access to highly lethal means of suicide

<mark>Remember that asking a person about suicide DOES NOT increase the likelihood that person will attempt suicide. Often, asking about suicide alleviates anxiety and helps the individual feel understood.</mark>

**Assess if patient:**
- Has suicidal risk behaviours such as;
  - Preoccupied with death
  - Threatening to hurt or kill oneself
  - Looking for ways to kill oneself by looking for lethal items like firearms, pills, sharp objects, ropes, poisons or other means
  - Giving away of possessions

<!-- page: 33 -->

- Visiting family members and bidding them *final* 'farewell
- Making arrangements for funeral, will
- Sudden acts of recklessness or engaging in risky activities that may endanger life
- Feeling or expressing that life is not worth living
- Verbalizing that everyone would be better off without them
- Self-medicating including alcohol and/or other substances
- **Self-harm behaviour**
- If there is a suicide note and current suicide intention
- Family history of suicide
- Past suicide attempt
- Mode /lethal weapon used
- Sudden behaviour change to someone who has been very depressed
- Chief motivator for suicide ideation /thoughts and attempt
- Availability of a suicide plan
- Social withdrawal
- Writing a suicidal note
- **Screen for suicide risk using the Suicide Risk Assessment Form** <mark>Suicide Risk assessment form annexure Page 99</mark>

# GRIEF AND BEREAVEMENT

## DEFINITIONS

**Bereavement**
- Is a period of mourning and grief after a loss of a loved one.

**Grief**
- Is a normal natural response to loss that involves physical, emotional, somatic, cognitive and spiritual response to actual or threatened loss of a person, thing or place that is emotionally attached to a particular person.
- Example: relationship breakup, loss of health, loss of a job or financial stability, miscarriage, loss of loved one in death etc.

## STAGES OF GRIEF

- The following are 5 stages of normal grief as elaborated by Kubler-Ross:
  - o Shock and Denial: person displays disbelief
  - o Anger: person becomes frustrated, irritable and angry about the loss
  - o Bargaining: person negotiates as an attempt to prolong life
  - o Depression: person may start showing clinical signs of depression or just sadness
  - o Acceptance: realization that death is inevitable and acceptance of experience
- Normal grief resolves and acute symptoms gradually lessen within one to two weeks. However some people may experience an abnormal course of grief with symptoms lasting over weeks.
- If grief symptoms persist for more than 2 weeks assess for the symptoms of Major Depressive Disorder episode **(*please check signs and symptoms of MDD*)**.

## COMPLICATED GRIEF

A client is considered as having complicated grief if
- Grief does not progress as expected. The intensity and duration symptoms and stages of grief are prolonged and dramatically interfere with a person's ability to function.

## Types of complicated grief

- Cumulative grief
  - o Is when one experiences a second loss while still grieving a first loss
- Exaggerated Grief
  - o Present with an overwhelming intensification of normal grief reactions that may worsen over time characterized by extreme and excessive grief reactions.

<!-- page: 36 -->

- Prolonged/Chronic grief
  - o Strong grief reactions that do not subside and last over a long period of time with no progress towards feeling better or improving level of functioning

# DRUG INTERACTIONS AND ANTI-DEPRESSANTS & HAART
Most first line HAART do not interact with anti-depressants
1. Nevirapine may decrease levels of fluoxetine
2. Ritonavir increase levels of amitriptyline and fluoxetine

4. Fluvoxamine increases levels of PIs & NNRTIs

# **BIPOLAR AFFECTIVE DISORDER JOB AID**

## **ASSESSMENT**

## **PSYCHO-EDUCATION**

Inform the family and patient (not when in acute manic state) about the following:
- Bipolar is a condition that tends to involve extreme mood swings seen over a matter of months, weeks, or over many days.
- Feeling very depressed, fatigued, extremely energetic, irritated and overly excited is the nature of the disorder.
- Relapses need to be prevented by recognizing when symptoms return, such as sleeping less, spending more money or feeling much more energetic than usual and coming back for treatment when these occur.
- A person in a manic state lacks insight into the illness and may even enjoy the euphoria and improved energy. Therefore, carers must be able to recognize the early signs of mania and seek for medical help as soon as possible.
- Alcohol and substance use have an impact on the disorder, therefore should be avoided.

- Encourage reactive social networking
- Educate family to avoid **Highly Expressed Emotions** towards the patient

  - Maintain a regular sleep cycle (e.g. going to bed at the same time every night, trying to sleep the same hours as before illness).

# **COMMON CHILDHOOD PSYCHIATRIC CONDITIONS JOB AID**

## **ATTENTION DEFICIT HYPERACTIVITY DISORDER (ADHD)**

## **PSYCHO-EDUCATION**

**Capacitate the parents/caregivers on the following;**

- **The nature of ADHD:**
  - Setting up very firm and clear routines.
  - Having daily and weekly routines visibly displayed.

- **Setting up a good reward system for good behaviour.**
  - Reward systems must suit the hyperactive child and it should be immediate and frequent rewards.
  - Once the child has earned points or rewards, never take them away for bad behavior.
  - Bad behavior should be modified.
  - Keep rewards and discipline systems totally separate
  - Inform parents to never ignore bad behavior. Hyperactive children do not just give up if bad behavior is ignored

- **Learn how to speak to a hyperactive child.**
  - If you want them to change bad behavior, phrase it as something they should do and not something to stop. Example: instead of saying “stop running”, say “walk”.
  - When you need to talk to the child, make sure you have his/her attention first.

- **Psychoeducation;**
- Encourage parent on inclusive education for the child
- Provide psychological and social support
- Refer according to stepwise management

## **CONDUCT AND OPPOSITIONAL DEFiant DISORDER**

**Assess if:**
- The child is being bullied or abused.

- The child has sexually transmitted diseases, HIV/AIDS or pregnant
- The child is abusing alcohol or illicit drugs.

# **Inform parents and caregivers**

How to implement the following for appropriate behaviour change:
- Be consistent about what the child is allowed and not allowed to do.
- Practice positive reinforcement; praise or reward the child after good behaviour: and respond to problem behaviours as prioritized.
- Find a way to avoid harsh confrontations or foreseeable difficult situations.
- Start behavioural change by focusing on observable behaviours that you think the child can do.
- Put off discussions with the child until you are calm.
- Never physically or emotionally abuse the child. Make punishment mild and less frequent compared to praise. Example: withholding rewards like fun activities after the child did not behave properly.
- Teachers should be part of the intervention. They can ensure the child is seated in front of the class and receives appropriate attention e.g. breaking assignments into smaller pieces.

## SEXUAL ASSAULT JOB AID

- Establish rapport
- Use a calm tone of voice and maintain eye contact
- Explain what you are going to do at every step.
- Let the survivor tell her/his story the way she/he wants to, in his/her own pace.

# TOTAL SCORE

Instructions – How to core the PHQ-9

**Major depressive disorder is suggested if:**
- Of the 9 items, 5 or more are checked as at least ‘more than half the days’
- Either item a. or b. is positive, that is, at least ‘more than half the days’

Other depressive syndrome is suggested if:
- Of the 9 items, a., b. or c. is checked as at least ‘more than half the days’
- Either item a. or b. is positive, that is, at least ‘more than half the days’

Also, PHQ-9 scores can be used to plan and monitor treatment. To score the instrument, tally each response by the number value under the answer headings, (not at all=0, several days=1, more than half the days=2, and nearly every day=3). Add the numbers together to total the score on the bottom of the questionnaire. Interpret the score by using the guide listed below.

## Guide for Interpreting PHQ-9 Scores

| Score | Recommended Actions |
| --- | --- |
| 0-4 | Normal range or full remission. The score suggests the patient may not need depression treatment. |
| 5-9 | Minimal depressive symptoms. Support, educate |
| 10-14 | Major depression, mild severity. Use clinical judgment about treatment, based on patient's duration of symptoms and functional impairment. Treat with antidepressant or psychotherapy. |
| 15-19 | Major depression, moderate severity. Warrants treatment for depression, using antidepressant, psychotherapy or a combination of treatment. |
| 20 or higher | Major depression, severe severity. Warrants treatment with antidepressant and psychotherapy, especially if not improved on monotherapy; follow frequently. |

## Functional Health Assessment

**Functional Health Assessment**

The instrument also includes a functional health assessment. This asks the patient how emotional difficulties or problems impact work, things at home, or relationships with other people. Patient responses can be one of four: Not difficult at all, Somewhat difficult, Very difficult, Extremely difficult. The last two responses suggest that the patient's functionality is impaired. After treatment begins, functional status and number score can be measured to assess patient improvement.

For more information on using the PHQ-9, visit www.depression-primarycare.org
